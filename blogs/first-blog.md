### 1. The Exact Formula for Dollar Gamma Exposure ($\text{GEX}_{1\%}$)
In the Black-Scholes model, **Gamma** ($\Gamma$) measures how fast an option's Delta ($\Delta$) changes for every 1-point move in the underlying index ($S$):

$$\Gamma = \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial S} = \frac{\phi(d_1)}{S \cdot \sigma \sqrt{T}}$$

To calculate the exact **dollar notional value of S&P 500 futures** that market makers must hedge for a **1% move in SPX** at strike $k$, institutional desks compute:

$$\text{GEX}_{1\%, k} = \Gamma_k \times \text{OI}_k \times 100 \times S^2 \times 0.01$$

## 4. The Math of 0DTE Strategies: Fixing the Iron Condor EV Trap {#0dte-strategy-math}

**Direct Answer:** A traditional symmetric 0DTE Iron Condor carries **negative expected value (EV)** if traded mechanically: collecting `$3.00` on a 10-point wide SPX spread risks `$7.00`, requiring a **$>70\%$ win rate** before commissions and slippage just to break even. ### Why Naive 0DTE Iron Condors Bleed Accounts
Suppose SPX is trading at **`7,700` at 10:15 AM ET**. A retail trader sells a standard 10-point wide Iron Condor at roughly 1 standard deviation ($\approx 68\%$ probability of remaining inside the range):
* **Sell `7,655 / 7,645` Put Credit Spread** + **Sell `7,745 / 7,755` Call Credit Spread**
* **Total Net Credit Collected:** `$2.80 to $3.00` (`$280–$300` per contract)

Here is the mathematical trap: if you risk **`$700` to make `$300`**, your theoretical breakeven win rate is:

$$\text{Breakeven Win Rate} = \frac{\$700}{\$700 + \$300} = 70.0\%$$

If your short strikes sit at the 68% probability boundary—and you factor in `$0.15` of bid-ask slippage plus the fact that a single afternoon gap move hits max loss—**holding symmetric 0DTE Iron Condors to 4:00 PM ET has negative mathematical expectancy.**

### How Our Desk Engineers Positive Expectancy Across 3 Setups

#### Strategy A: The GEX-Anchored Credit Spread / Skewed Iron Condor (Positive GEX Only)
